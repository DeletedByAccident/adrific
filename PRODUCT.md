# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary audience is **Finnish SMBs and growth-stage companies** — owners, founders, and marketing leads who are evaluating an agency for either paid-ad management, custom software, or both. Their context: they are comparing AdRific against other agencies and freelancers, often skeptical, and they decide largely on *evidence of capability*. They land on the site (desktop during a workday, sometimes mobile) asking one question — "can these people actually build something exceptional, or is this another template shop?" — and the site has to answer it before they read a word.

AdRific Oy is Helsinki-based ("based in Finland, serving the world"). The audience is Finnish-first in market, but the surface currently speaks English; bilingual fi/en is a plausible future need, not a current requirement.

A secondary reader is the **referral / repeat prospect** who arrived via word of mouth and needs the site to confirm the impression they already have: that AdRific is the real thing.

## Product Purpose

AdRific is a Finnish digital agency offering **premium ad management and custom software development**. This site exists to win SMB and growth-company clients by *proving, through its own craft*, that AdRific builds digital work others can't. It is simultaneously the pitch and the portfolio: the most convincing case study AdRific can show is the site itself.

The site is a **reference / portfolio surface for the company** — it displays what AdRific has designed, built, and shipped (its own products plus selected client work). It is not a services-for-hire sales funnel; there is no "what we build for you" pitch and no RFP/conversion machinery. Its job is to **establish AdRific as a premium, technically exceptional studio by showing the work**, with a soft, always-available way to get in touch — never a hard sell. Success looks like: a visitor who arrives skeptical leaves thinking "how did they make this?" The bar is not "looks professional"; it is "could not have been made by a template."

## Brand Personality

**Bold, intricate, code-native.** AdRific should come across as a studio of builders who are genuinely ahead of the curve — confident and ambitious, but with the substance to back it. The defining quality the client asked for is *programming-artistic*: the site should feel made, not assembled — intricate, generative, precise, out-of-the-box, the work of people who clearly write code at a high level.

Voice is bold and direct without hype-for-hype's-sake. Energy and ambition lead, but every bold claim is earned by the craft on screen. The wow comes from technical artistry — computational, code-native intricacy — never from emoji, pastels, gimmicks, or noise. It is impressive the way a beautifully engineered thing is impressive: you sense the rigor underneath.

## Anti-references

What this must **not** look or feel like:

- **Generic SaaS template** — the current look: dark-navy body, gradient text, gradient hero, identical icon-heading-text cards, the Outfit font. The AI-default landing page. The single most important thing to escape.
- **Cheap / discount agency** — busy, clip-art-y, "we do everything for everyone," stock-icon clutter, budget energy.
- **Stuffy corporate** — cold enterprise tone, stock handshake/skyline photography, soulless and risk-averse.
- **Gimmicky startup** — emoji-led, pastel, playful-for-its-own-sake, gimmicks standing in for substance.
- **Traditional / normal / common** — anything that reads as a conventional business website. AdRific is a programming studio; the surface must feel code-native and crafted, never off-the-shelf. If a visitor could mistake it for "a website," it has failed.

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the portfolio.** Show, don't tell. Practice what we preach: a digital studio's own site is its strongest case study, so every section and interaction should *demonstrate* capability rather than claim it. A line of copy that says "we build exceptional work" is worth less than a moment of craft that proves it.
2. **Out of the box, on purpose.** Reject the template. The target reaction is "how was this made?", not "which kit is this?" Strangeness and ambition are in service of craft — a visitor mistaking this for any other agency site, or for a SaaS starter, is the failure mode.
3. **Bold, but earned.** Lead with energy and confidence, but never let bold tip into gimmick. The impact must come from real engineering and computational craft, not from emoji, pastels, or loudness. Substance is the source of the swagger.
4. **Built by people who code.** The aesthetic is programming-native and artistic — intricate, generative, exact. The surface should feel authored by engineers with taste, where the detail rewards a second look and nothing is decorative-by-default.
5. **Let the work speak.** This is a reference, not a sales pitch — no "what we build for you" services section, no RFP funnel. The work carries the persuasion; a soft "get in touch" stays available for anyone who wants it, never pushed.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.1 AA** is the target: ≥4.5:1 body-text contrast, ≥3:1 for large text and meaningful UI, full keyboard operability, and visible focus states — even on a heavily art-directed surface. Ambition is not an excuse to fail contrast; verify it.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` is honored on all motion.** Because this brand leans on generative / computational visuals and entrance choreography, every animated effect needs a reduced-motion alternative (crossfade or static), and content must never be gated behind a motion that may not fire.
- Intricate visuals must **degrade gracefully**: the page reads and converts even where WebGL/canvas is unavailable or JS is slow. The craft enhances an already-complete, legible page; it never replaces it.
